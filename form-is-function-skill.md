# Form Is Function

## The First Principle

Design and function are not two categories to balance or trade off against each other. They are a single interrogation applied to any object, component, or decision until its value is defensible across both dimensions simultaneously — or until it earns its impermanence honestly as a working model.

**This is not a preference. It is the prior condition.**

A choice originating in form (aesthetic, visual, spatial) is held to the exact same standard as a choice originating in function (interaction, behavior, logic). Neither category protects a choice from interrogation. Neither has a default relevancy weight. A green button that is green because the other buttons are green has failed. A component placed because an action needed a home has failed. Neither failure is more acceptable than the other.

---

## The Interrogation

When any design or product decision is live — regardless of how small, how obvious, or how conventional it appears — apply the following interrogation before committing:

**What is this thing, fully?**
Not what it is assumed to be. What it actually is. Strip the label. What does it do, what does it present, what does it hold?

**What has been used as this thing?**
What precedents exist across domains — not just UI, not just this product? What else has solved a similar problem in a surprising or more complete way?

**What could it be?**
Not what it conventionally is. What are the edges of what it could do, hold, present, or become? Don't close this question early.

**Does its design optimize or add value to its use?**
Does the form of this thing make its function better, more legible, more delightful, more surprising, more precise? If not — why does the form exist?

**Does its function borrow anything from its form?**
Can the way this thing looks, moves, or sits in space unlock behavior or interaction that wouldn't otherwise exist? What functions does the design open the door to?

**What would be surprising or genuinely valuable here?**
Not clever for its own sake. Surprising in a way that serves. Valuable in a way that isn't obvious from the conventional version of this thing.

**What does this component expect of itself at module level? At app level?**
A button is not isolated. What does it connect to? What does it echo? What does it set up for later? What is it already reaching toward?

**Can this choice tie to something already in progress — by design, by function, or both?**
Cross-reference what's being built. Look ahead in the project. Can this decision create a bridge rather than an island?

## On Impermanence

Every choice is editable. A working model earns its place by being useful long enough for a better answer to surface. The whiteboard doesn't fail when it's replaced — it succeeded by lasting exactly as long as it needed to.

Committing to impermanence is not the same as being uncommitted. A choice is made with full interrogation and full conviction, and it remains in place until the interrogation produces something better. This is not permission to avoid deciding. It is the operating condition under which every decision remains open to improvement.

Do not protect a choice from re-interrogation by citing the effort that went into it. The effort is not the value. The value is what the choice produces.

---

## What Failure Looks Like

A choice has failed the interrogation if any of the following are true:

- It was made because the adjacent choices were the same
- It was made because conventions suggested it
- It was made because one dimension (form or function) was satisfied and the other wasn't checked
- It was made to complete a pattern rather than to produce value
- It was placed because something needed to go somewhere
- Its impermanence was never considered

When a failure condition is identified, do not soften it. Name it. Strip the decision back to foundation and re-run the interrogation. That is the default mode.

---

## Application in Sessions

When this skill is active, every design or product suggestion, recommendation, or analysis must:

1. Apply the interrogation before committing to any choice
2. Surface what the conventional or assumed version of the choice would be — and then immediately question it
3. Identify at least one dimension where the choice could produce value that the conventional version does not
4. Acknowledge when a choice is impermanent and name what would need to be true for it to be replaced
5. Never treat form and function as a tradeoff. If a choice satisfies one and not the other, the interrogation is not finished.

If a session participant (user or agent) proposes a choice that has not been interrogated — even a small one, even an obvious one — flag it. The principle applies universally. There are no exceptions for convenience, for speed, or for apparent simplicity.

---

## The Interrogator

For sessions where active interrogation pressure is required — not just methodology, but a present voice that challenges choices in real time — hand the resisting choice to the `interrogator` subagent.

The interrogator does not decorate decisions. It applies the interrogation and does not release a choice until it has earned its place or earned its impermanence.
